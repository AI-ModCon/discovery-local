  Overview and Outcome
  The Genesis Mission: Hands-on Hackathon is designed with one clear outcome in mind: every Genesis Mission RFA Award team leaves with a strong starting point for integrating their scientific workflow into the Genesis Mission Platform. The Hackathon will provide RFA Award teams with focused access to technical expertise, open capabilities and services, and computing resources to advance near-term project needs, resolve or characterize technical barriers, and identify common requirements that help inform continued development of ModCon and AmSC.
  A Track for Every Stage of Development
  Whether your RFA Award team is just getting oriented or already deep into development, this Hackathon is built to move you meaningfully closer to advanced development and real-time production. Time is critical, so we've structured the Hackathon to meet every team exactly where they are. Because each RFA Award team is at a different stage of maturity, the RFA Award teams will participate in three tracks based on self-selected project readiness. Therefore, rather than a one-size-fits-all agenda, the Hackathon is organized across three progressive tracks — Foundations, Intermediate, and Advanced.
  The three tracks are designed to flow naturally into one another. Teams starting at the Foundations track will progress through all three tracks across the three days, building a complete foundation by the end of the event. Teams that start at the Intermediate track can skip the foundations hands-on sessions and dive straight into fine-tuning and agentic workflows before advancing further. Teams following the Advanced track will spend the full three days immersed in the most complex, cutting-edge topics relevant to their work.
  Registration
  This Genesis Mission: Hands-on Hackathon is open to invited members of the ModCon, AmSC, and RFA Award teams (2 participants per team), along with select DOE representatives. Space is limited and will close on September 30, 2026 (or earlier if maximum capacity is reached). Registration is required. Note, registration does not guarantee admittance. Do not make travel plans until you have received a formal confirmation, confirming your participation in the Hackathon. You will then receive information on site access, as well as a final Agenda and Hackathon materials.
  Register here: https://events.cels.anl.gov/event/942/overview
  Why It Matters
  This Gensis Mission: Hackathon is a strategic accelerant for the RFA Award teams and the Genesis Mission Platform. By the end of the three days, every team will have hands-on experience working code, defining clear next steps for critical scientific achievements. Both ModCon and AmSC will gain clarity on common needs, requirements, and short-term blockers across the Gensis Mission RFA Award Team portfolio, allowing both projects to advance at a faster rate.
  🌱 Foundations TrackHands-on sessions covering the scientific and technological foundations.
  F1. Data preparation and access of DOE data collections
  Lead Instructor: Jeren Browning
  Short Description: Participants will explore how to discover and access relevant DOE data collections and prepare them for analysis, modeling, and AI-enabled research, in order to gain a better understanding of available resources, common access considerations, and practical steps for turning raw or distributed data into usable research inputs.
  F2. Using the Integrated Research Infrastructure (IRI) - API to launch and manage jobs on HPC Systems
  Lead Instructor: Tom Uram
  Short Description: Participants will learn the IRI API to submit and manage jobs on the various DOE HPC systems via a Restful API.
  F3. Creating a scientific, engineering benchmark to assess AI advantage
  Lead Instructor: Fernando Llorente
  Short Description: Participants will review the reasons for early establishment of benchmarks, overview key components when creating a benchmarks, describe how ModCon tools can be used to evaluate progress on benchmarks, and allow for time for teams to brainstorm and start developing internal benchmarks.
  F4. Discovery engine and machine actionable cards
  Lead Instructor: Rafael Ferreira da Silva
  Short Description: Participants will be introduced to the Discovery Engine, a multi-agent system for resource discovery, workflow composition, and grounded hypothesis generation, whose foundation is a set of machine-actionable cards, i.e., structured, self-describing representations that both humans and agents can reason over and act upon. Participants will examine the underlying architecture, learn how cards expose GM resources through standard protocols such as MCP, and prototype cards for resources from their own domains.
  F5. Agentic coding
  Lead Instructor: Murat Keceli
  Short Description: Participants will be introduced to agentic coding tools including Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, and other emerging options, along with VS Code as an IDE for integrating AI-assisted development into everyday coding workflows, followed by a hands-on exercise using Agentic Coding Tools to explore and modify a small project, practice effective prompting and iteration, and learn practical workflows and best practices they can apply to their own projects.
  F6. Best Practices for Scientific Workflows (BPSW) design patterns
  Lead Instructor: Robert Underwood
  Short Description: Participants will be introduced the notion of best practices for scientific workflows as reusable patterns that represent approaches to addressing specific challenges in scientific workflows, in order to learn to search the best practices catalog in the Discovery Engine to learn from past experience building AI systems for science in the context of their workflows.
  F7. Secure and safe AI practices
  Lead Instructor: Dave Florey
  Short Description: Participants will learn about new threats, risks, and failures that can occur in the era of Agentic AI, including best practices, skills, and tooling that can be used to reduce the likelihood of unintended failures and increase robustness.
  ⚙️ Intermediate TrackHands-on development for RFA Award teams ready to build and refine
  I1. Agentic scientific workflows (e.g., Academy, LangGraph, and beyond)
  Lead Instructor: Yadu Nand Babuji
  Short Description: Participants will learn to design, deploy, and manage scalable agentic scientific workflows using frameworks such as Academy and LangGraph, gaining hands-on experience building and deploying autonomous systems across DOE lab computing environments through example agentic workflows in materials, biology, and chemistry.
  I2. Implementing design patterns for scientific workflows
  Lead Instructor: Robert Underwood
  Short Description: Participants will build upon the notions of best practices for scientific workflows by emphasizing the trade-offs that exist when implementing patterns, allowing deeper knowledge of how to quantify and engineer solutions regarding when and how to deploy patterns when multiple choices exist under constraints that appear in their workflows.
  I3. Agentic Uncertainty Quantification
  Lead Instructor: Natalie Isenberg
  Short Description: Participants will learn about available Genesis Platform tools for agentic uncertainty quantification and assessment of scientific models and workflows.
  I4. Benchmarking-driven model development and designing a Genesis Mission leaderboard competition
  Lead Instructor: Nahn Tran
  Short Description: Participants will review core benchmarking concepts, explore how the Genesis platform supports benchmark creation on CodaBench, and demonstrate a tool designed to help build benchmarks for Genesis, as benchmarking is essential for demonstrating measurable AI advantage and for engaging the broader multidisciplinary Genesis community.
  I5. Getting started with pretraining foundation models on DOE supercomputers
  Lead Instructor: Murali Emani
  Short Description: Participants will be introduced to the software environments available on various supercomputing facilities to facilitate model pre-training, as well as learn best practices for effective campaigns on HPC systems.
  I6. AI-ready data and reusable artifacts
  Lead Instructor: Victor Walker
  Short Description: Participants will explore approaches for creating machine-actionable, well-described, traceable artifacts that can be evaluated, connected, and reused across organizations and scientific domains, answering the question--what makes a scientific data product useful to the next model, agent, experiment, research team or partner, rather than only to the group that created it?
  I7. Agentic tools for AI-ready data
  Lead Instructor: Jean Luca Bez
  Short Description: Participants will learn about two ModCon / GM Platform tools -- DSAGT, an interactive data preparation framework, and AIDRIN, a data evaluation tool -- and their integration in agentic pipelines, with a majority of time devoted to deploying such a pipeline on participants' data.
  🚀 Advanced TrackAccelerated progress for RFA Award teams pushing the frontier
  A1. Multi-modal pretraining
  Lead Instructor: Sandeep Madireddy
  Short Description: Participants will learn how to build and train multimodal models for their own scientific data, from preparing paired multimodal datasets to integrating specialized encoders across different modalities and connecting them to a shared decoder, such as a language model. Hands-on exercises will cover multimodal pretraining, modality alignment, multimodal post-training, and practical strategies for efficiently training and scaling these models on leadership-class computing systems.
  A2. Scaling multi-agent workflows
  Lead Instructor: Kyle Chard
  Short Description: Participants will be introduced to practical approaches for scaling multi-agent scientific workflows, from coordinating multiple specialized agents and tools to executing larger workloads efficiently on HPC systems, thus learning key concepts in orchestration, parallel execution, resource management, and monitoring, with hands-on examples showing how multi-agent workflows can be structured and scaled for different applications.
  A3. Profiling and optimization of AI training workloads for science at scale
  Lead Instructor: Sam Foreman
  Short Description: Participants will learn how to scale AI workloads on HPC systems, including how to instrument, profile and identify bottlenecks in workloads, and strategies to apply to effectively scale AI workloads.
  A4. What to know before you deploy publicly
  Lead Instructor: Ilya Katz
  Short Description: Participants will join a panel that brings together tech transfer and intellectual property experts to outline essential guidance project teams need to know before deploying or publishing data, assets and artifacts. Learn how to ensure alignment with partnership, intellectual property, and asset governance expectations. It will help teams understand the key guardrails, approval pathways, and bestpractice considerations that support responsible and compliant public release.
  A5. Scaling laws
  Lead Instructor: Neil Getty
  Short Description: Participants will learn how to determine whether a scientific model improves with more parameters (N), data (D), and compute (C) and how to design a minimal Kaplan-style ablation that produces a defensible scaling curve on a modest allocation. In the hands-on portion, participants will fit and extrapolate power laws to real training-run data.
  A6. Adaptive Models: Building Drift-Aware Continual Learning Systems
  Lead Instructor: Andrew Ayres
  Short Description: Participants will learn how to detect concept drift in data streams and build models that adapt in real time without full retraining. Hands-on exercises will cover configuring drift detectors, selecting continual learning strategies, orchestrating monitoring loops, and running experiments on image classification tasks. You'll work with the APEIRON framework to build an end-to-end adaptive pipeline that detects when model performance degrades and automatically adapts—then extend it to your own problem.
